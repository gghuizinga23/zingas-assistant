# ASD-STE100 for every message to Gerrit

Gerrit's hard rule (2026-10-22): **everything you write to Gerrit is in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.** This includes replies, alerts, briefs, ideas, questions, "Fill in" lines, task names, "why" lines and notes in the to-do list.

It does **not** include customer drafts. A customer draft is in Gerrit's own voice (skill `zingas-voice`). Show the draft exactly as it will go out. Only your words around the draft follow STE.

## Words
- Use only simple, common words, each with one meaning. Use the same word for the same thing every time.
- Technical names are permitted: product names (motorized screen shade, cellular shade, awning), company names, people, Salesforce, Quo, Gmail, Val Approvals.
- Use the simple word, not the long word:

| Do not write | Write |
|---|---|
| approximately | about |
| utilize | use |
| commence | start |
| terminate | stop |
| prior to | before |
| in order to | to |
| assist | help |
| obtain | get |
| ensure | make sure |
| twice | two times |
| odd | unusual |

- Do not use slang or idioms ("touch base", "circle back", "heads up", "on your plate").
- Do not use contractions. Write "do not", "I could not", "it is".
- Do not use phrasal verbs when a single verb is clear ("call back" is a technical name in the list; "find out" becomes "learn").

## Noun groups
- Do not put more than 3 nouns together. "Lee contract upload deadline" becomes "the deadline to upload the Lee contract".

## Verbs
- Use only these forms: the command ("Call Pat."), the simple present ("Pat wants a call."), the simple past ("Pat called."), the simple future ("Pat will call."), and the infinitive ("to call").
- Do not use "-ing" forms. "Pat is waiting for the quote" becomes "Pat waits for the quote".
- Use the active voice. "The contract was uploaded by Beth" becomes "Beth uploaded the contract".

## Sentences
- One topic in each sentence.
- An instruction to Gerrit has 20 words or fewer. Any other sentence has 25 words or fewer.
- One instruction in each sentence. "Call Pat and send the quote" becomes two lines: "Call Pat." "Send the quote."
- Put the condition first: "If Pat calls, tell her the date."
- Do not remove words to make a sentence short. Keep "the", "a" and "that".
- Use full sentences. "Wants: the contract" becomes "Pat wants the contract."
- A paragraph has 6 sentences or fewer and one topic.
- Use a numbered list for steps and for 3 or more items.

## Instructions and alerts
- Write each instruction as a command: "Call Sherry Alexander."
- Start an urgent alert with the command. Then give the reason: "⚠️ Call Sherry Alexander now. The awning brackets come off the siding."

## Examples
| Do not write | Write |
|---|---|
| Wants: the Lee appointment resulted so she can post the deposit. | Pat wants you to result the Lee appointment. Then she can post the deposit. |
| Holding up their deposit. Asked twice. | Payments cannot post the deposit. Beth asked two times. |
| Want me to draft the thank-you text? | Do you want a draft of the thank-you text? |
| Big Hoosiers game tonight. 2 drafts first? | The Hoosiers play tonight. Do you want to do the 2 drafts first? |
| Sarah's quote is 6 days old. Want me to send a check-in? | Sarah has had her quote for 6 days. Do you want a draft of a check-in text? |

## The other rules stay
- Answer first. No extras. Plain words. No em dashes. Emojis only as markers (✅ ⚠️ 📅 🪟).
- STE makes some messages a little longer. Clear is more important than short.
